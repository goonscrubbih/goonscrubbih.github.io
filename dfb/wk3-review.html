"vs. league" is all-play: each team's score against all eleven others, every week. Buttons are seventh in points scored.

Three of these teams are good. TYJT and the BEAGLES would be 3–0 against almost any schedule, and Tampa has the second-most points in the league. Benjamin's Buttons are seventh in points and below .500 against the field, which is normally how you describe a 1–2 team.

In fairness, Buttons had Jahmyr Gibbs, who scored 41.4, the best week by anyone. On Tuesday their manager told the chat that taking Gibbs first "feels like cheating." Wrong culprit. The cheating is the schedule.

Monday night showed how it works. Buttons needed six points. The safe move was Kyle Monangai; their manager started Dontayvion Wicks instead, and explained it in the chat: "I want them points." Wicks scored 5.2 and, by his owner's own review, wasted two touchdown targets. Monangai scored 3.1 on the bench, so the risky call was the right one. Buttons won by 10 with 35 points left on their own bench. That is the season in one night.

This week that swap alone wins the game by 11.8. Add Jakobi Meyers, who scored 19.4 on the bench while three starters failed to reach four, and Rage's best lineup beats Buttons by 29.5.

The rest of the roster did its job. Kirk Cousins, picked up Saturday for Justin Herbert, scored 22.2, and Drake London, Garrett Wilson and Ja'Marr Chase all cleared 24. Rage is 0–3 and 12–21 against the field. The receivers are fine. The defensive coordinator is on notice.

The Matchup of the Week: the nine other guys won it

Last week's preview was titled "Josh Allen and Nine Other Guys." Allen scored 17.5, his worst week of the season, and he is now listed questionable with a knee. The nine other guys won by 31.9: Christian McCaffrey scored 21.6, Jaylen Warren 20.6, and even the kicker got 16.

The preview picked the BEAGLES by single digits. Right team, wrong by 23 points on the margin. Its better prediction was the one it made by accident: these two never play close games. After six meetings the series is 3–3, and the closest game is still 6.36.

Pukachu went 1–10 against the field. Wan'Dale Robinson, picked up Sunday morning, scored 18.7 on the bench while Brian Thomas started and scored 1.8. Puka Nacua scored zero for the second week running, but from the bench this time, which counts as progress.

The 0–3 club

The Taylords are the defending champions, and this loss was not a lineup problem: their best possible lineup still finishes 17 short. De'Von Achane left with a torn ACL and is on IR after surgery, the worst news of the week for anyone. Tampa's manager summed up the rest in the chat, surprised that the team that drafted Kenneth Walker could be 0–3.

JohnnyBoVonny has the luckiest career record in league history, 34–22 on below-average scoring, and 2026 is collecting on it. Last week they benched Patrick Mahomes and lost by 2.5. This week they started him. He scored 15.9, Keenan Allen scored 18.3 on the bench, and the Patriots defense scored minus one. That is the lowest score of the week and a 5–28 season against the field, six games worse than anyone else.

Rage is covered above, at length, against its publisher's wishes.

Around the league

Peeka Egbuka lost Jaxson Dart to IR, picked up Drew Lock on Wednesday, dropped him Friday for Geno Smith, and Geno scored 26.0. First win, second-best score, third quarterback.

Stay Questionable set a perfect lineup, including the right call at quarterback: Tyler Shough (23.8) over Joe Burrow (22.6). Bijan Robinson scored 35.3.

TYJT was also perfect. Brock Purdy threw four touchdowns, and Brock Bowers, listed out with a knee last week, scored 27.6. On Tuesday traffikhan put Juwan Johnson on the trade block, right after Johnson scored 23.3. Selling high, apparently.

Tampa Tropics dropped Kyle Pitts on Tuesday. Buttons, in the chat: "Bro he's about to pop just wait!" Tampa: "Probably now that I dropped him."

Saquan Deebos Chubba scored 135.16, the most of any loser, and drew the only team that cleared 160. The Sex Panthers got 26.2 from George Kittle and ran into Peeka's best week.

How this was put together

Scores, lineups and every rostered player's points come from Sleeper, pulled Sept 30 after Week 3 closed. "Best possible lineup" fills each lineup slot with the highest scorer on the roster, using hindsight on purpose: it measures what was available, not whether a call was reasonable at kickoff. Injury statuses are Sleeper's as of publication.

Chat quotes are from the league chat, lightly trimmed. Transactions are from the league's own log. This league uses rolling waivers, not FAAB, so there are no bids to report.
