K&Y Matchup of the Week · Week 3

Josh Allen and Nine Other Guys

The two highest-scoring teams in the league meet, projected 2.5 points apart. They have played five times and never once been close, so this will presumably be a 40-point blowout. Sorry in advance.

Sept 23, 2026 I Choose You Pukachu! (1–1) vs The BEAGLES (2–0)

Every other game this week is either close and bad or good and lopsided. This one is the only game that is both good and close: the highest combined projection on the board and the second-smallest gap, which is the entire job description of this award.

These two drafted back-to-back all night from slots four and five, which means every round they spent an hour watching each other take the player they wanted. Now they get to settle it.

It is also the week's most philosophically confusing matchup, for reasons explained below, involving a quarterback who is personally responsible for a quarter of his team's offense and a receiver whose injury is slowly migrating down his own body.

Probable starters, with this season's points per game — two games, so treat every number as a rumour. Q is questionable. Totals are the sum of those averages, which is not a prediction, it's an average wearing a prediction's clothes. Lineups can change until kickoff.

These are the same team, viewed from two directions

Here is the confusing part. The preseason draft board says Pukachu's roster is about nine points a game better than the BEAGLES'. Our own draft review handed Pukachu an A+ and had the BEAGLES middle of the pack.

This season says the BEAGLES are fourteen points a game better. They are 20–2 against the full field — best in the league by two games — and scoring 152.3 a week.

Average those two opinions and you get a game that is 2.5 points apart. This is either a rigorous synthesis of prior expectation and observed evidence, or two wrong answers holding each other up in a trenchcoat. The desk has chosen to find it rigorous.

The BEAGLES: one man and a support group

Josh Allen is averaging 38.24 points a game. That is a quarter of everything his team scores. He has scored 35.66 and 40.82 and has not yet had a bad week, which is the kind of sentence that ages like milk in a hot car.

Behind him the roster gets more democratic. Christian McCaffrey is at 18.2 and DeVonta Smith at 18.0. Then it thins out fast: Rhamondre Stevenson went 14.50, then 3.60. Quentin Johnston projects into a flex spot on 3.05 points a game, which he earned with back-to-back performances of 3.70 and 2.40, a consistency this desk genuinely respects.

The bench is where their week gets decided. Jalen Coker scored 33.80 in Week 1 and is questionable with an ankle. Jonah Coleman scored 14.80 and is questionable with an ankle. Dallas Goedert is doubtful with an MCL. If the ankles clear, the BEAGLES get materially better; if they don't, Quentin Johnston starts, and we all live with that together.

They have also won twice while leaving 32.80 points on the bench in Week 2 alone, the most of any winner. Winning ugly is a skill and their manager has four years of practice at it — more on that below.

Josh Allen is a quarter of his team's offense. The other quarterback on that roster is Malik Willis, which tells you how the contingency plan reads.

The BEAGLES, 2–0, 20–2 against the field

Pukachu: the best roster nobody can get out of the garage

Four seasons, a .568 all-play — second-best in league history — a 26–30 record, zero titles, and a best-ever finish of 8–6. This is a manager who has been quietly excellent at the part nobody hands out trophies for.

The fourth pick of the 2026 draft was Puka Nacua, and he has so far produced 12.40 points and a did-not-play. The 117th pick was Travis Kelce. This is the entire Pukachu experience in two data points.

Week 2 was the whole career in one afternoon. Pukachu lost to Tampa by 39.26 — and their best possible lineup would have scored 167.58, beating Tampa's perfect lineup by 0.12. They had the winning team. They fielded a different one. Specifically they started Lamar Jackson (14.80) over Matthew Stafford (26.98), which was completely defensible, and started Puka Nacua, who did not play at all, which was less so.

Nacua is now questionable with a groin, having been listed out with a hip on Monday. The injury appears to be migrating south. At the current rate he is questionable with an ankle by Sunday and fully healthy by Halloween.

The rest of the roster is genuinely frightening: Ashton Jeanty at 21.5 a game, Trey McBride at 21.3, and Travis Kelce — the 117th pick of the draft, currently their second-best tight end — at 17.6 and coming off 25.10. A team whose FLEX is Travis Kelce is not a team with a talent problem.

One roster note this desk owes you: our own projection model wanted to fill the other flex with Xavier Smith, who has scored 0.00 and 0.00, ahead of Tre Tucker, who scored 22.90 last week. The model reads a preseason ranking sheet the way your uncle reads a 2019 fantasy magazine. The fight card above starts Tucker, because a human looked at it.

Two managers, one problem, opposite directions

This is the rare game where both managers are living proof of this desk's favourite argument, and they are living it from opposite ends.

The BEAGLES' manager is 33–23 all-time on a .491 all-play — the second-luckiest record in league history. Last season he went 6–8 while scoring like a .312 team — one of the four worst scoring seasons in this league's records — and he has a championship and a runner-up finish to his name anyway.

Pukachu is 26–30 on a .568 all-play and has nothing to show for it.

So of course in 2026 the BEAGLES are also the best-scoring team in the league. The man who spent four years winning without the points has acquired the points. This is deeply unfair and should be reported to someone.

What actually decides it

Three flex-shaped injuries and one quarterback.

If Josh Allen has his third 35-point week, the BEAGLES probably win regardless of anything else on this page, because nobody on Pukachu's roster can be relied on for 38. If Allen has a normal quarterback game — call it 20 — the BEAGLES are suddenly an ordinary team with Quentin Johnston in the lineup.

Then the ankles. Coker at 33.80-in-Week-1 level swings the BEAGLES up by ten or more over Johnston. And on the other side, a healthy Puka Nacua is a 14-point-a-game receiver; a questionable one has produced 12.40 and a did-not-play, and Pukachu has already been burned once by putting him in the lineup and finding out on Sunday.

The pick here is the BEAGLES by single digits, on the theory that the quarterback advantage is 18 points wide and the rest roughly cancels. The desk reserves the right to be extremely wrong, as established last week, when the game it said would be decided by a hamstring was decided by a man catching one pass in 37 snaps.

How this was put together

Rosters, injury statuses and every 2026 score come from Sleeper, pulled Sept 23. Each team's projection is the best legal lineup (QB, 2 RB, 2 WR, TE, 2 flex, K, DEF) under two models, averaged: the preseason cheat sheet used on draft day, converted to a per-game figure, and this season's actual points per game. Questionable players are discounted, out and IR players zeroed.

Two honest caveats. First, the per-game numbers rest on two games, which is barely evidence at all. Second, last week's preview quoted a 35-point gap that was season-long roster value rather than a one-week projection, and the game finished 5.02 apart; the per-game framing on this page exists specifically because of that mistake. Head-to-head results are computed from this league's own 2022–2025 matchup archive.
