The K&Y Matchup · Week 1

Both of them still have a ring

The last two champions of this league open the season against each other — and the last time it mattered, one of them ended the other's year on the way to the trophy.

There are six games on the board in Week 1 and only one of them puts a championship belt on both sides of the field. The Taylords won it all last season. Benjamin's Buttons won it the year before. Between them they own two of the last three titles and two of the three highest scoring averages in the history of this league.

They have met five times. Three of those went to the wire. One of them — and this is not a typo — was decided by six tenths of a point.

And the most recent meeting that carried any weight was a knockout. Week 16 of 2024, the semifinal: Buttons hung 180.5 on the Taylords, the highest score either side has ever put up against the other, ended their season on the spot, and won the championship two weeks later. Tbunzz answered the only way available to him, by winning the whole thing himself the following year.

Now they open against each other, with the projection inside 22 points, four questionable starters between them, and one Taylords receiver the rest of the fantasy world thinks is badly underrated here.

The injury report

Week 1 rooms tag liberally, and this league is carrying forty-one designations across twelve rosters. Most are noise. Four of them are starters in this game, and two of those are worth watching.

Malik Nabers — knee

Buttons · WR2 · questionable

Listed with a knee, specified as ACL. He is the second receiver in a lineup that needs both. If he sits, Buttons starts an unranked replacement and the 22-point edge evaporates on that slot alone.

Sam LaPorta — hip

Taylords · TE · questionable

The Taylords' only startable tight end. Behind him is nothing. Buttons already wins the tight end slot by 12 with LaPorta healthy; without him it becomes a rout at the position.

D'Andre Swift — undisclosed

Buttons · RB2 · questionable

Less alarming, but Buttons' backfield behind Gibbs is thin and the fallback is Tony Pollard, who is already in the lineup at flex.

Zay Flowers — undisclosed

Taylords · WR1 · questionable

No body part given, which usually means precautionary. The Taylords have Romeo Doubs and KC Concepcion behind him — a real drop, but not a hole.

What decides it

Jayden Daniels

Taylords · QB5 · the biggest single edge on the field

Thirty-six points clear of Kyler Murray, and one of only two slots where the Taylords hold a decisive advantage. If Tbunzz wins this game it is almost certainly because his quarterback outscored his opponent's by more than the flex spots cost him.

Jahmyr Gibbs

Buttons · RB2 · first pick of the draft

The 1.01, and the answer to Achane. He gives Buttons the best single healthy player in the matchup and cancels the Taylords' quarterback edge on his own if he hits his ceiling.

The flex problem

Taylords · Alec Pierce · WR37

Buttons counters the Taylords' first flex with Terry McLaurin and takes 17 points out of the slot. This is where the Taylords' draft was thinnest and there is no help on the bench — Romeo Doubs and KC Concepcion are the alternatives.

Parker Washington

Taylords · second flex · the number this preview is least sure of

Our board has him WR41 and projects him at replacement level. He finished 2025 as WR27 overall, and over the back half of that season he posted a 28.9% target share and 3.78 yards per route run. In the games after Jacksonville's Week 8 bye — with Brian Thomas Jr. and Jakobi Meyers both on the field — he led the team at a 31% target share.

If that is who he is, this projection is wrong by about thirteen points and this game is a coin flip rather than a 22-point edge.

The dead roster spot

Buttons · Josh Jacobs · not active

Still rostered, now formally listed inactive with a groin issue and no timeline. On a five-man bench that is a fifth of Buttons' flexibility doing nothing — in a week where three of his own starters are questionable and he may need the pivot.

The read

Tbunzz owns the strangest line in this league's record book: the best all-play percentage of anyone, ever, and a career record of exactly .500. Over four seasons he has scored like a contender and finished like a coin flip, because the schedule kept handing him the week's second-best total. In 2025 the variance finally broke his way and he won the whole thing.

Buttons is the opposite animal. Thirty-and-twenty-six, one title, and a waiver record that reads like a man who refuses to accept his roster as drafted — 171 adds and nearly five thousand points added over four years. He does not out-draft people. He out-manages them in October.

Which is why the projection understates this one. Buttons is favoured by 22 points on paper and holds five of the ten slots, but the Taylords have the single biggest positional edge on the field, and Buttons is the one carrying the more dangerous injury. In a format this shallow, the first Sunday is decided by whoever's third-best starter shows up.

And the projection has a soft spot, which is worth saying out loud rather than burying. Parker Washington is the single most disputed player in this game. Our board is on the low end of a range where the market, the industry and our own pre-draft research all sat higher — the notes in our own files called him a legit sleeper winning the Jacksonville target battle, and the board ranked him below Jakobi Meyers anyway. On the bear side, his breakout stretch lined up with Travis Hunter's absence, and Hunter's Week 1 role is genuinely unsettled: the snap-count reporting has him drifting to cornerback while Jacksonville's own depth chart lists him as a starting receiver. That one variable decides whether the Taylords are 22 points light or level.

Both of these men have stood at the end of a season holding the trophy. Neither has beaten the other when it counted in two years. Week 1 does not settle any of that — and they will both play it like it does.

How this one got picked

Six candidates. Every matchup was scored on combined projected output and on how close the projection was, then weighed against head-to-head history and what is actually at stake. The scoring was re-run after the week's roster moves and with injury designations applied; the order did not change.

The tightest projection on the board was not this game — Tampa Tropics and Rage are separated by seven points, and Tampa is 4–0 all-time in that series with last year's meeting decided by 1.5. It is a genuinely great matchup and it is the numbers pick.

It went to the champions instead for two reasons. This game has the billing that needs no explanation, and three of its five meetings were decided by single digits — and the man selecting the Matchup of the Week plays in the other one. Week 1 is a poor time to hand yourself the marquee.
