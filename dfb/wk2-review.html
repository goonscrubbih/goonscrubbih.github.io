Week 2 in review

Five Teams Lost With the Win on the Bench

Last week every game went to the better score. This week, five of the six losing teams had a winning lineup on their own roster and started a different one. Two of them were a single lineup swap away.

Sept 22, 2026 Week 2 final · all 16 games played

Week 1 was the fairest week this league has had: the six best scores won and the six worst lost, in order. Week 2 answered with the other kind of week. Take every losing team, put its best possible lineup on the field from the players it actually had, and compare that against what its opponent actually scored. Five of the six would have won. In Week 1 the same test flipped one game.

The only loser this doesn't apply to is Stay Questionable, who lost by 28.30 and could not have closed the gap with anyone on the bench.

One swap each

JohnnyBoVonny started Drake Maye (8.02) over Patrick Mahomes (28.98) and lost by 2.50. Maye played the whole game — 55 snaps, this wasn't an injury — and Mahomes threw for 382 yards and three touchdowns on the bench. Make that swap and JohnnyBoVonny wins by 18.46. Amon-Ra St. Brown's 35.20 went down with it.

This is the manager this desk has written up as the luckiest record in league history: 34–22 on scoring that ranks below average. Two weeks into 2026 the luck has run the other way. JohnnyBoVonny is 0–2 and 5–17 against the full field, second-worst in the league.

The Taylords started Alec Pierce (2.10) over KC Concepcion (8.20). That's six points in a game lost by 5.02 — the swap alone wins it by 1.08. More on the Taylords below.

Pukachu needed all three, and would have won by twelve hundredths

It was also built mid-week: Dalton Schultz, picked up Thursday, caught 12 balls for 26.00, and Rashod Bateman, picked up Friday, scored 21.80. The two waiver adds scored 47.80 between them. Tampa won by 39.26.

And I Choose You Pukachu! nearly beat that perfect lineup anyway. Their best lineup scored 167.58, higher than Tampa's by 0.12, but it needed three changes at once: Matthew Stafford (26.98) over Lamar Jackson (14.80), Tre Tucker (22.90) over Puka Nacua, and Brian Robinson (8.70) over David Montgomery (4.40).

The K&Y preview said Zay Flowers's hamstring would decide this one. Flowers didn't play. Tbunzz had already benched him for Matthew Golden, so the lineup the preview called "as currently set" is the game that got played.

That gap was far smaller than the preview implied. The 35-point edge it gave the Sex Panthers was season-long roster value, not a one-week projection, and one Sunday squeezed it to 5.02. Marvin Harrison started for the Panthers and scored 0.00 on one target in 37 snaps, Jacksonville's defense scored 2.00 and J.K. Dobbins 3.60. On the other side, Jaylen Waddle went from 1.20 last week to 21.80 and Kenneth Walker scored 23.80. CeeDee Lamb's 35.30 is what decided it.

So the Sex Panthers — 20–36 all-time, the worst record in the league's history — beat the defending champion. And The Taylords were one bench swap away from winning it.

The Taylords are 0–2 by a combined 9.40 points: 4.38 to Buttons, 5.02 to the Panthers. Both weeks, a winning lineup was on the roster. They have the best all-play record in league history and a career .500 record, and 2026 has added two more close losses to that. Rage: third-best score in the league, 0–2

This desk is written from Rage's side of the league, and this week that means reporting on its own manager's lineup decisions.

Last week Rage went 0–11 against the field. This week Rage scored 134.78 — third in the league, 9–2 against the field — and lost to the second-best score, The BEAGLES' 146.02. Josh Allen's 40.82 was the second-best performance in the league, and it was on the other team.

It was still winnable. On Thursday, Rage dropped Cade Otton to pick up the Green Bay defense, then started Green Bay (7.00) over Las Vegas (16.00). Travis Etienne also started for 7.10 while Devaughn Vele scored 11.40 on the bench. Make both changes and Rage scores 148.08 and wins by 2.06. Neither one wins it alone. The one real improvement: Dalton Kincaid, who sat for 18.00 last week, started and scored 22.50.

The BEAGLES won despite leaving 32.80 points on their own bench, the most of any winner. DJ Moore scored −0.10 before leaving after 23 snaps and is now listed out with a shoulder injury. Through two weeks the BEAGLES are 20–2 against the full field, the best in the league by two games.

The rest of the gap was. Hunter Henry (7.00) sat while Colston Loveland started at tight end for 1.30, for the second week running a near-zero. Woody Marks (8.50) sat while Michael Wilson started for 3.80. Those two swaps, with Dart left in the lineup, score 107.40 — enough to beat Buttons' 106.08. Peeka had the week's low score and is 0–2 and 4–18 against the field, last in the league.

Benjamin's Buttons are 2–0 on the tenth-best score of the week. Their waiver move made the difference: Bryce Young, claimed for Bo Nix, scored 24.08 with Kyler Murray, last week's −0.38, on the bench and now listed out with a concussion. How this was put together

Final scores, lineups and every rostered player's points were pulled from Sleeper on Sept 22 after Week 2 closed. "Best lineup" fills QB, two RB, two WR, TE, two RB/WR/TE flex, K and DEF with the highest scorers on each roster. It uses hindsight on purpose: it measures how many points were on the roster, not whether the lineup call was reasonable at the time. The piece says so wherever an in-game injury, rather than a lineup choice, caused the zero.

Injury statuses are Sleeper's as of publication, not what was known at kickoff. Snap counts and box-score lines are from Sleeper's weekly stats feed. Transactions are from the league's own log.
